# One-Page Business Launch Site Pilot

**Purpose:** a separate offer for an owner who confirms they need a first or replacement web presence. Do not merge this with the existing ₦75,000 enquiry-path repair offer.

**Status:** pricing and scope hypothesis; not yet validated in a paid conversation.
**Test price:** **₦125,000 fixed**, split 50% to begin and 50% after preview approval, before public launch.
**Delivery target:** 7 calendar days after deposit and receipt of all required content, access, and decisions.
**Illustrative build:** `/demo/launch-site` is a fictional solar-business concept for internal preview only. It is not a client project, has no live contact route, and must not be represented as completed client work.

## Fit and qualification

Offer this only after a real conversation confirms that the business wants a website and has someone available to approve content. A missing website URL in a directory is not proof that no official site exists or that the owner wants one. If the owner says they already have a site, stop this pitch and ask what they actually need.

“Recently opened” is not required for this offer. Do not say or imply that the business has just opened unless a credible, dated source confirms that fact and the source has been checked immediately before use.

## Included

- One responsive, single-page site for one business and one primary audience.
- Sections for the business summary, up to three services/products, supplied trust material, service area, and contact details.
- One clear contact action agreed with the owner: WhatsApp, tap-to-call, email, or a simple form using an owner-controlled destination.
- Basic page title, description, social preview, HTTPS setup where the chosen host supports it, and a final mobile/desktop check.
- One consolidated revision round after the preview.
- Source code and deployment handover; the client controls their domain, hosting account, and credentials.

## Not included

Logo/brand design, original photography, extensive copywriting, ecommerce, booking/payment systems, CRM, paid ads, ongoing SEO, monthly maintenance, business email, additional pages, or third-party subscriptions. Domain registration and hosting are paid directly by the client to the chosen provider and are not fronted by Bryan or included in the ₦125,000 fee.

The project clock starts only when the deposit and agreed content are received. Delayed content or approvals move the delivery date. No promise is made about rankings, traffic, leads, enquiries, or revenue.

## Acceptance and payment

1. Confirm the owner, audience, services, contact destination, and exact scope in writing.
2. Send a short acceptance note and collect ₦62,500 before work begins.
3. Build a preview using client-provided facts and assets; do not invent testimonials, certifications, years in business, client counts, or opening dates.
4. Apply one consolidated revision round.
5. Collect the remaining ₦62,500 before launch and hand over code, accounts, and a short update guide.

If the scope changes, pause and agree a new written price and schedule before doing the extra work.

## Permission-first opening message

Only use after personally checking the business-controlled web presence and the channel/recipient. Do not use the missing-website signal from a Places record as proof.

> Hi [Name/team], I’m Bryan, a software developer. I was checking [business]’s public online presence and couldn’t confirm an official website; I may have missed it. If you’re considering a simple page for your services and customer enquiries, would it be useful if I sent a short outline? No problem if it isn’t a priority.

Do not attach a mockup, unsolicited audit, or proposal in this first message. If they say yes, ask what the page needs to do before presenting the fixed scope and price. If they say no or ask not to be contacted, stop and record that outcome.

## Price hypothesis and evidence

The ₦125,000 price is an experiment, not a market average. A 2026 Nigerian agency pricing page lists landing pages from ₦150,000 and five-to-eight-page sites from ₦500,000 ([Brela pricing](https://www.brela.agency/pricing)). A Nigerian 2026 pricing guide estimates ₦80,000–₦150,000 for a one-page landing page and ₦150,000–₦300,000 for a small business site ([DPX Digital Network](https://dpx.com.ng/how-much-does-a-web-designer-charge-in-nigeria/)). Another 2026 guide gives a broader ₦95,000–₦300,000 band for a one-page business site and explicitly notes that these are planning ranges, not regulated prices ([Elvis Onunwa](https://elvisonunwa.com/pricing-costs/website-design-cost-in-nigeria)). These are providers' own published prices, not independent transaction data or proof of local willingness to pay.

Do not discount automatically. Ask what the owner needs, quote the same scope, and record the objection (price, trust, no need, timing, existing provider, DIY, or other). Revise the price only after evidence from real conversations.
